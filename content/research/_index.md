# Research

I am interested in understanding how evolutionary, demographic and ecological processes interact to shape genetic diversity, local adaptation, and the evolutionary trajectories of natural plant populations. My research combines population genomics, quantitative genetics, field experiments, and historical herbarium collections to investigate local adaptation, demographic history, and biological introductions.

---

## Temporal Genomics and Demographic History of Capeweed

*Arctotheca calendula* | Capeweed

Understanding how demographic history and selection interact to shape genomic variation through time and across geographic ranges is a central challenge in evolutionary biology. However, reconstructing evolutionary trajectories remains challenging in complex non-model systems because evolutionary change often unfolds over timescales exceeding direct observation. Herbarium records are an exceptional resource for recovering this missing spatiotemporal dimension.

Here, I incorporate population and temporal genomics to reconstruct the introduction history and understand rapid adaptation of *Arctotheca calendula* in Australia. Despite its broad global distribution and evidence of local adaptation, how capeweed became established globally and what genomic changes have occurred since introduction remain unknown. Using historical herbarium genomes, I investigate spatiotemporal changes in genetic diversity and how these trajectories have been shaped by genetic drift, gene flow and selection.

Beyond understanding the demographic history of capeweed, I am also building phased chromosome-level genome assemblies to construct a comprehensive capeweed pangenome, providing a genomic framework for investigating structural variation across populations.

## Flowering Phenology and Assortative Mating

*Lythrum salicaria* | Purple Loosestrife

How does variation in reproductive timing influence mating opportunities and evolutionary trajectories?

Using common-garden experiments and population genomic approaches, I investigate flowering synchrony, assortative mating, and local adaptation in purple loosestrife.

## Population Genomics for Ecological Restoration

*Calamagrostis* species | Native Grasses

How can genomic information improve ecological restoration practices?

My research uses genomic tools to investigate genetic differentiation, hybridization, and relationships between wild and commercially sourced populations of native grasses in northern Canada.

These findings help inform species identification and the selection of appropriate plant material for restoration.

## Local Adaptation Across Environmental Gradients

*Quercus rubra* | Northern Red Oak

How does environmental variation shape genetic and phenotypic differentiation?

Combining quantitative genetic and genomic approaches, I investigated patterns of differentiation across environmental gradients in northern red oak populations around Lake Superior.
